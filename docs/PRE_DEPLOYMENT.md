# Pre-deployment checklist

Items that **must be resolved before the site goes public**. Each is a known gap, recorded rather than fixed so far.
Tick an item only when it is fixed and verified, and record how.

| # | Item | Why it matters | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tile routes have no per-user rate limit.** `/api/tiles/{aid}/{kind}/{year}/…`, `/api/imagery/tiles/{aid}/{year}/…` and `/api/tiles/{aid}/context/{layer}/…` are protected only by input validation, the disk tile cache, single-flight fetching and the backend-wide Earth Engine concurrency cap (`TT_EE_TILE_CONCURRENCY`, bounded queue) — not by `app/ratelimit.py`. | One client can request many uncached tiles (all zoom levels, every year and layer), occupying the shared Earth Engine slots and quota for every other user, and growing the tile cache. Cheap for cached tiles, expensive for uncached ones. | **Fixed 2026-10-25**: tiles that need Earth Engine (cache misses) are limited per client, `TT_RATE_TILES_PER_MIN` (default 120); cached tiles are never refused. Test: `test_uncached_tiles_are_rate_limited_per_client_but_cached_tiles_never` |
| 2 | **One integrity-suite run timed out on first load** (`page.waitForFunction: Timeout 45000ms exceeded` waiting for the map and the four featured markers). It happened right after a backend restart, while the backend was measuring the featured sites' area context for the first time (Earth Engine Restricted Mode); it could not be reproduced afterwards (the app booted in 4.6 s) and the rerun passed. | A first visit right after a deploy or restart may hang or show the offline preview until the backend's first Earth Engine work finishes. Root cause not established — possibly the cold `/api/health` waiting on Earth Engine initialisation (measured 7.3 s against the frontend's 8 s timeout) combined with startup Earth Engine work. | Open (recorded 2026-10-10) |
| 3 | **Per-client rate limits become per-proxy without `--proxy-headers`.** Behind a reverse proxy, uvicorn sees every user as the proxy's address unless started with `--proxy-headers --forwarded-allow-ips=<proxy>`; all users then share one counter. Measured 2026-10-10: each new area selection makes 4 compute-group requests (register, preview, reference context, imagery decision), so the default 120/min would allow ~30 new areas per minute across ALL users. | Normal use by several people at once could hit 429 ("Too many requests"); the reference card then shows "unavailable". | **Fixed in the Docker image 2026-10-25**: its start command uses `--proxy-headers --forwarded-allow-ips=${TT_FORWARDED_ALLOW_IPS:-*}`. Verify on the chosen host that two clients get separate counters |
| 4 | **Move the AI chat (Gemini) to a paid tier.** The prototype uses the Gemini API free tier. Google's terms (checked 2026-10-10): on unpaid services "Google uses the content you submit … to improve and develop" its products and "human reviewers may read, annotate, and process" input and output; "Do not submit sensitive, confidential, or personal information to the Unpaid Services". | User questions could be read by human reviewers and used for training. | Open (recorded 2026-10-10) |
| 5 | **EU / UK / Switzerland users require the paid tier.** Terms: "You may use only Paid Services when making API Clients available to users in the European Economic Area, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom." (The API itself is available there; the free tier may not serve those users.) | Serving those users from the free tier breaches the terms. | Open (recorded 2026-10-10) |
| 6 | **Gemini API users must be 18+.** Terms: "You must be 18 years of age or older to use the APIs", and the Services may not be used in API Clients "directed towards or likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18". | A public Expo stand or school audience may include minors: the chat must be disabled or restricted there. | Open (recorded 2026-10-10) |
| 7 | **Set the chat limits from the project's real Gemini limits — and mind the reset times.** The defaults (6/min and 60/day per client, 8/min and 200/day for the backend) are conservative guesses: Google publishes no fixed free-tier figures; they are shown per project in AI Studio. **Reset mismatch:** TerraTrace's daily chat counters reset at midnight **UTC**, Google's daily quota at midnight **Pacific** (07:00 or 08:00 UTC), so for 7–8 hours a day the two count different days: TerraTrace may allow requests Google refuses (they then fail with 429, handled: one request, no retry, then `TT_CHAT_COOLDOWN_S` of immediate "busy" answers). Measured 2026-10-10: Google answered 429 after about 20 requests in one day. Settings: `TT_RATE_CHAT_PER_MIN`, `TT_CHAT_PER_CLIENT_PER_DAY`, `TT_CHAT_GLOBAL_PER_MIN`, `TT_CHAT_GLOBAL_PER_DAY`, `TT_CHAT_COOLDOWN_S`. | Too high: most users get "busy"; too low: the chat refuses more than it needs to. | Open (recorded 2026-10-10) |
| 8 | **Gemini free-tier latency is erratic.** Measured 2026-10-10 (gemini-3.8-flash, thinking "low"): the same kind of request took 6.6 s, 14 s, 60 s+ and once 150 s (a server-side 504); 6 of 8 calls in one run exceeded 60 s. `TT_CHAT_TIMEOUT_S` is 60 s; a timeout is reported as "not available right now", never retried. Thinking level "minimal" is rejected (HTTP 400) by this model. | A visitor may wait a minute and get no answer. A paid tier (item 4) may behave differently — re-measure there. | Open (recorded 2026-10-10) |
